## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & BUSINESS MODEL

### 1.1 The Product Definition

Pakhi is an enterprise Data-as-a-Service (DaaS) platform. It ingests raw meteorological data from satellites, reanalysis, and forecast models, runs them through high-speed, JIT-compiled pipelines, and transforms them into tradeable signals for commodities, power grids, and climate risk derivatives.

**The product is an early-warning system for weather-driven financial risk.** It translates chaotic, multi-gigabyte meteorological data into clean, calibrated probability outputs (e.g., "72% probability of Florida freeze in 5 days → elevated LONG probability for Orange Juice futures"). Final execution logic always stays on the client side.

### 1.2 The Dual-Stack Ecosystem

* **The Engine (`triples-sigfast`):** The underlying, open-source computational library — Numba JIT-compiled kernels (LLVM-backed) with GIL-free parallel execution and on-disk cached compilation, delivering near-C speed for rolling features, EMAs, anomaly detection, and pipeline primitives. Out-of-core streaming of multi-gigabyte weather models is handled by the dask/xarray integration layer. Owned and developed in-house.
* **The Vehicle (Pakhi):** The commercial wrapper. Financial clients purchase the *outputs* (alpha, risk mitigation, and actionable intelligence) generated by the engine.

### 2.2 Core Modules & APIs

* **`pakhi.src` (Data Connectors):** Automated ingestion for NOAA GFS, ECMWF ERA5, GOES-16/17 Satellite, Open-Meteo, CME Weather derivatives, and Yahoo Finance. Includes `GFSConnector.archive()` for as-published historical runs (see Backtest Integrity, §3).
* **`pakhi.features` (Feature Engineering):** Leverages `triples-sigfast` JIT kernels for blazing-fast rolling averages, EMA, anomaly detection, teleconnections (ENSO/NAO), and climate indices (HDD/CDD).
* **`pakhi.models` (Forecasting Engine):** Common interface supporting PyTorch LSTMs, LightGBM/XGBoost tabular models, Gaussian Processes for uncertainty, and Bayesian Model Averaging (BMA) ensembles. Trained on reanalysis ground truth; *evaluated and deployed on operational forecast inputs.*
* **`pakhi.signals` (Trade Generation):**
  * **Freeze Signal:** Orange Juice (OJ) / Natural gas futures spike probability.
  * **Power Signal:** ERCOT/PJM grid demand vs. wind supply price deviations.
  * **Hurricane Signal:** Catastrophe bond and natural gas risk pricing.
  * **Ensemble Disagreement Index (flagship):** Quantifies ECMWF-vs-GFS/GEFS divergence for the forecast region and lead time; historically, model disagreement is itself a volatility and risk-pricing opportunity — independent of which model is "right."
  * **Temperature Index Signals (CME):** Cooling Degree Day (CDD) / Heating Degree Day (HDD) / freeze-index futures — the forecast-vs-consensus spread is directly tradeable.
* **`pakhi.risk.backtest` (Backtesting Engine):** Full transaction-cost adjustments (e.g., `commission_bps=5`, `slippage_bps=10`) applied to all reported results. **Inputs must be as-published operational forecasts, never reanalysis, at trade time.**

## 3. ENTERPRISE BULLETPROOFING & RISK MITIGATION

* **Multi-Redundant Ingestion (Auto-Failover):** Weather APIs fail frequently. If `gfs.latest()` times out, Pakhi seamlessly falls back to `ecmwf.latest()` or `openmeteo.latest()`, dynamically re-weighting the ML ensemble and logging the failover. A pipeline must never return an empty DataFrame.
* **Latency & Streaming via `triples-sigfast` + dask/xarray:** Pakhi processes incoming GRIB/NetCDF files in micro-batch chunks as they download (dask out-of-core), with JIT-accelerated feature kernels, bypassing the need to wait for multi-gigabyte file completions. Target: signal published within **60 seconds** of the operational run becoming available (measured from model publication timestamp, not from NOAA release — we can't beat the centers; we beat the clock from their data to our signal).
* **Automated Model Drift Calibration:** Pakhi continuously evaluates real-time Brier Skill Scores (BSS) against ECMWF/GFS baselines. If predictive skill drops below institutional thresholds, the system triggers alerts for automated model retraining.
* **Backtest Integrity (As-Published Discipline):** *Lookahead bias is the #1 way weather-quant backtests become fiction.* ERA5 is a retrospective product — it uses after-the-fact observations to correct and smooth the data. Backtesting on reanalysis at trade time is "trading with tomorrow's newspaper." Pakhi's rule:
  * **Model training:** ERA5 reanalysis is used only as historical ground truth so models learn real atmospheric physics and teleconnections.
  * **Backtesting & execution:** strictly point-in-time operational forecasts — `GFSConnector.archive()` returns each GFS run exactly as published at that hour on that date. Every backtested trade records the forecast run ID and publication timestamp.
  * **Never** is reanalysis fed into the signal generator as a simulated trade-time feature.
  * Any published backtest must state its data provenance (forecast archive, not reanalysis).
* **Regulatory Guardrails (CFTC/SEC):** Pakhi is positioned as a "Weather Intelligence Provider." Commercial API outputs are **probabilistic forecasts and risk analytics**, not trade recommendations; final execution logic is pushed to the client's internal systems. This framing is necessary but not sufficient — before the first commercial contract, **obtain legal counsel** on: (a) adviser/CTA registration exposure when signals feed client algos, (b) terms-of-service disclaimers of suitability, (c) liability for downstream trading losses, (d) data-licensing compliance for any commercial feeds.

  * Train baseline XGBoost models for the specific wedge market (e.g., ERCOT demand, or CDD/HDD settlement).

* **Production:**
  * Run a live paper-trading portfolio using Pakhi's signals for 60 days, with transaction costs applied.
  * Publish real-time performance (Sharpe, max drawdown, BSS vs. ECMWF/GFS) on quant forums and LinkedIn, **with full data-provenance disclosure** (as-published forecasts, not reanalysis).

### Phase 3: Commercial API & Enterprise Pitch (Months 6–8)

**Goal:** Secure the first enterprise DaaS contracts.

* **Development:**
  * Wrap the predictive engine in a low-latency FastAPI/WebSocket layer.
  * Implement user authentication, API key management, and rate limiting.
* **Production/Sales:**
  * Target boutique commodity hedge funds and proprietary energy desks reached through warm intros.
  * Offer a 14-day trial of the live API stream.
  * The Pitch: *"Pakhi delivers calibrated weather-to-signal intelligence with documented skill over the ECMWF/GFS baselines and a trustworthy, as-published backtest methodology. Signal latency from model publication: under 60 seconds. Final execution stays on your side."*

### Phase 4: Horizontal Scaling (Months 9–12)

**Goal:** Expand instrument coverage and deploy deep learning models that have earned their place.

* **Development:**
  * Roll out advanced Transformer and Graph Neural Network (GNN) models **only after they demonstrate out-of-sample skill over ECMWF/GFS baselines in production evaluation** — no client is ever sold on unproven model architecture.

## 5. SUCCESS METRICS & KPIs

### 5.1 Current Performance Status (Honest Baseline)

As of v3.1: **there are zero real-world out-of-sample results.** Every metric previously published (e.g., 1.34 Sharpe, 4.13% return on OJ_FUTURES) was generated on 100% synthetic data and exists purely to validate software architecture, pipeline execution, and terminal outputs. **No synthetic number is a performance claim.** First real performance data arrives with Phase-2 paper trading.

### 5.2 KPI Framework (Value Metrics, Not Engineering Vanity)

| Metric | Target (Launch, Phase 2 paper trading) | Target (Year 1) |
|---|---|---|
| **Forecast skill vs. ECMWF/GFS baseline** (CRPS/BSS improvement, wedge variables, 1–7 day lead) | BSS ≥ 0.10 on wedge tail events | BSS ≥ 0.15, CRPS ≤ baseline on core variables |
| **Trading performance** (cost-adjusted Sharpe on live paper trading, commission+slippage applied) | ≥ 1.0 (after costs) | > 1.5 |
| **Signal hit rate** (direction-of-move, cost-adjusted) | ≥ 55% | ≥ 58% |
| **Time-to-signal** (model run published → signal delivered) | ≤ 60 s | ≤ 30 s |
| **System uptime (API)** | 99.9% | 99.99% |
| **Backtest provenance** | 100% of published backtests use as-published forecast archives | Same — non-negotiable |

### 5.3 Why These KPIs

* **BSS/CRPS vs. ECMWF/GFS** is the only forecast metric institutional buyers trust. Raw RMSE without a baseline is noise.
* **Cost-adjusted Sharpe / hit rate** reflect what a client actually earns after friction — the only numbers that survive a second meeting.
* **Time-to-signal** is our honest edge: we cannot out-forecast the NWP centers, but we can move their data through our pipeline faster and turn it into calibrated market information faster than any incumbent.
* **Backtest provenance** is the trust contract. One revealed reanalysis-based backtest destroys the company.

## APPENDIX A: THE PRODUCT & BUSINESS MODEL (ELABORATION)

### A.1 What Exactly Is Pakhi?

Pakhi is an Enterprise Data-as-a-Service (DaaS) Platform for quantitative finance, energy trading, and reinsurance markets.

You are not selling software licenses, and you are not asking institutions to install an application. You are selling latency, predictive accuracy, and actionable financial intelligence.

The product is structured as a dual-layer ecosystem:

* **The Open-Source Core (The Lead Magnet & Technical Moat):** A free, publicly available GitHub repository with the underlying processing engine and basic data ingestion pipelines. This is your technical resume. It proves computational rigor to skeptical quantitative researchers and establishes Pakhi as a baseline standard for weather-quant testing.
* **The Enterprise API & Terminal (The Actual Product):** A premium, managed cloud service. Hedge funds and energy desks pay for live REST/WebSocket streams of calibrated trading signals, sub-second grid-strain analytics, ensemble disagreement metrics, and access to a cloud-hosted as-published backtesting engine. They pay you to manage the infrastructure and complex data pipelines so their traders can act on finalized intelligence.

### A.2 The Business Model: Open-Core Data-as-a-Service (DaaS)

**Tier 1: Community Edition (Forever Free)**
* **The Offer:** Open-source access to the core engine, standard data connectors (NOAA, ERA5), and baseline ML models (XGBoost).
* **The Business Purpose:** Marketing and trust-building. Quants and academic researchers build local models with it, building brand authority and normalizing Pakhi's data architecture.

**Tier 2: The Managed Data API ($1,500 – $5,000+/mo)**
* **The Offer:** Subscription access to the live, low-latency API.
* **The Value:** Pre-computed trading signals, ensemble disagreement metrics, and proprietary ensemble forecasts from cloud-hosted models that have demonstrated baseline-beating skill. Clients pay for convenience, speed, and reliability of the auto-failover pipelines. Pricing tiered by instrument feeds (e.g., Agricultural futures vs. ERCOT power grids).

**Tier 3: Enterprise Infrastructure & Custom Models ($10,000+/mo or custom annual)**
* **The Offer:** Dedicated cloud instances, custom model training, and enterprise-scale as-published backtesting environments.
* **The Value:** Large funds blend Pakhi weather signals with their proprietary market data in a secure, SOC2-compliant environment, running multi-decade portfolio simulations in seconds. High-margin, sticky contracts — once a fund integrates the API into automated trading, churn approaches zero.
